# Orion Lahore: recording script for speech tests (Pashto, Urdu, English), 2026-11-13

**What this is.** Short, simple phrases and three short "call" scenarios, for a real speaker to read aloud and record, so that the speech reading can be tested on a known text. Each phrase is one clear sentence about everyday things a case file talks about (names, places, times, phone numbers, a car, money). Record them, upload the files into the Orion Lahore case, and the questions in `orion-speech-questions-20261113.md` can be asked of the transcripts; the reading is scored against this script (word error per recording).

**Please check before use.** The Pashto is written in standard Pashto script in the plainest form; it has **not** been reviewed by a native speaker (reviewer names, decision D10, are still owed). A native speaker should correct any phrase that is not natural for their own region before it is recorded, and the corrected text is the script the reading is scored against. Urdu and English are plain standard forms.

## How to record

* A phone voice recorder is enough. A quiet room, the phone about 20 cm from the mouth, one speaker at a time, normal speed, a short pause between phrases.
* **One phrase per file** for the 24 + 14 + 14 phrases (`ps-phrase-01.m4a`, `ur-phrase-01.m4a`, `en-phrase-01.m4a`, ...), and **one file per scenario** (`ps-call-01.m4a`, ...). Any of wav, m4a, mp3, ogg works. Do not edit or clean the audio.
* Say the digits one by one as written (zero three zero zero, one two three ...). Say the plate letters as letters.
* Use your own natural voice: men's and women's voices, and a phone held a little further away or a little noise in the background, are all useful. Say in the file note who recorded and in what conditions.
* Nothing in this script is real: the names, the numbers and the plate are made up.

## Three short calls (one file each language)

Each is about 20 seconds, spoken as a single call, in the speaker's natural pace.

**Pashto (`ps-call-01`):** سلام، زه احمد یم. نن ماښام پنځه بجې په بازار کې راشه. د موټر شمېره ایل ای اې یو دوه درې څلور ده. موږ سبا لاهور ته ځو. خدای دې په امان.
*(Hello, I am Ahmad. Come to the market at five this evening. The car number is L E A one two three four. We go to Lahore tomorrow. Goodbye.)*

**Urdu (`ur-call-01`):** سلام، میں احمد بول رہا ہوں۔ آج شام پانچ بجے بازار میں آ جاؤ۔ گاڑی کا نمبر ایل ای اے ایک دو تین چار ہے۔ ہم کل لاہور جا رہے ہیں۔ خدا حافظ۔

**English (`en-call-01`):** Hello, this is Ahmad. Come to the market at five this evening. The car number is L E A one two three four. We are going to Lahore tomorrow. Goodbye.

## What the questions can then show

With these recordings in the case: how many recordings there are and in which language, how long they are, which recordings mention the market, a number, Lahore or Peshawar (a spoken word, in any of the three languages), what the first segment of a recording says, and, for each recording, how far the reading is from this script (word error per recording, per language). That last figure is the honest measure of how well the product hears a real voice in real conditions.
